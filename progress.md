`great. can you create a software program coded in javascript that when given a present simple base sentence can then provide sentences in different tenses as you have?

Also can you explain why the tense s wat it is.`

  - see [tenses, aspect modality](https://academicmarker.com/grammar-practice/verb-functions/verb-aspect/is-verb-aspect-different-to-tense-and-modality/)
  
  - `tenses` | *fixed positions in time* **past, present** 
  
    *how?* | shown by making changes to the form of a verb
  
  - `modality` | *fixed positions in time* **future**
  
    *how?* | add a modal verb such as ‘will’ or ‘may’ to the verb phrase in a process called verb modality

    [academically for future tenses. In english we add a modal verb to the verb phrase](https://academicmarker.com/grammar-practice/verb-functions/verb-aspect/is-verb-aspect-different-to-tense-and-modality/)
  - `aspect` | *expresses periods of time or time relations.* 
           
    *how?* | making changes to the main verb or by adding new verbs to the verb phrase
  
    1. **simple** | *default fixed time*
    
    > *we **painted** the room* / `paint` | happened at a point in time, fixed
    
    2. **progressive** | *used to express a period of time between two often undefined points*
    
    > *we **were painting** the room* / `are painting` | over a period of time
    
    3. **perfect** | *is commonly used to expresses actions which are already completed but which retain some relevance to the present situation:*
    
    > *we **had painted** the room* / `have ??? painted` | ??? completed
    
    4. **perfect progressive/continious** | *is generally used to express events which are both continuous and completed*
    
    > *we **had been painting** the room* / `have been painting` | completed, over a period of time
    
    ??? we will have been painting tomorrow/for 8 hours.
